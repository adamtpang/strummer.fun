## The voicing

The whole trick is that the top voice barely moves. Hold `E4` through three
chords and let the bass do the walking. That stillness is the Avril 14th feel.

| Bar | Chord | Left hand | Right hand |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Am | A2 | A3 · C4 · E4 |
| 2 | Fmaj7 | F2 | A3 · C4 · E4 |
| 3 | C/G | G2 | G3 · C4 · E4 |
| 4 | E7 | E2 | G#3 · B3 · D4 |

Top voice across the four bars: **E4, E4, E4, D4**. That is the entire idea.

MIDI numbers if you would rather draw it in than play it (C4 = 60):

- bass: `45, 41, 43, 40`
- bar 1 and 2 chord: `57, 60, 64`
- bar 3 chord: `55, 60, 64`
- bar 4 chord: `56, 59, 62`

Function in A minor: `i · VI7 · III/5 · V7`. The G# in the E7 is the borrowed
leading tone, the one note that makes it ache.

## The form

At 72 bpm one bar is 3.3 seconds, so 32 bars lands at about 1:47. That is the
whole piece.

- **Bars 1 to 4** · left hand alone. Let it feel unaccompanied.
- **Bars 5 to 16** · add the right hand. Melody stays inside A natural minor,
  except over the E7 where you get the G#.
- **Bars 17 to 28** · same progression, right hand an octave up, or thinner.
  Change one thing, not three.
- **Bars 29 to 32** · take the melody away. End on Am, let the pedal ring out
  past the end.
